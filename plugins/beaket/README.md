# beaket

Cut the context of one decision down to what it needs, keep the decision as a record, and share records with your team end-to-end encrypted.

## Skills
- **decision-brief**: name a decision you must make; it reads Slack, Jira, Confluence, Notion, Google Meet notes and GitHub through your own connectors and gives you one page, each line linked to its source.
- **decision-record**: say what you chose, why and who you talked with; it drafts the record from the brief, you confirm, and it writes `decisions/<date>-<slug>.md`. A choice you answer on a card is kept from the card and your answer, with no draft to confirm.
- **what-to-decide**: call Beaket without naming a decision and it shows the one thing your team's records left open, the same one Beaket's page opens on, as a card; your answer is kept as a team decision and that thing is not offered again.
- **team**: make or join a Beaket team, invite someone, share or withdraw records, see the passports, and answer what leaves your machine. A machine can be in several teams; a repository names its team in `.beaket/team`, and a session reads and shares with that team only after you trusted that file there (`team use`).

## Install
In Claude Code 2.1.275 or later:
```
/plugin install beaket --marketplace beaket/plugins
```
In earlier versions, `/plugin marketplace add beaket/plugins`, then `/plugin install beaket@beaket`.

### Pin a release
For a security review, install from a release tag rather than the default branch:
```
/plugin marketplace add beaket/plugins#v0.14.1
/plugin install beaket@beaket
```
From a shell, `claude plugin marketplace add beaket/plugins#v0.14.1` does the same. An organization can allow only that source in its managed settings with `strictKnownMarketplaces: [{"source": "github", "repo": "beaket/plugins", "ref": "v0.14.1"}]`. Auto-update is off for this marketplace unless someone turns it on. Each release is tagged `v<version>` and its notes give the SHA-256 of `bin/beaket.mjs`; `node bin/beaket.mjs verify` prints the hash of the copy you have and checks that every skill's copy is the same. The plugin has no dependencies and installs nothing else.

## Beta
Teams are made on `stg.beaket.com`, Beaket's beta server. Beaket will run on `beaket.com` later. Set another server with `team create --server <URL>` or `BEAKET_URL`.

## What leaves your machine
- **Your sources.** The skills read them through the connectors your Claude Code already has, with your own access. What they read goes to the model as in any Claude Code session. The plugin sends none of it to Beaket; it has no connector or server of its own for sources.
- **Your records, only in a team, only when you confirm.** `bin/beaket.mjs`, of which each skill carries a copy in its `scripts/`, encrypts a record on your machine before it leaves:
  - The team key (32 random bytes) is made on a member's machine and never leaves members' machines unwrapped. `hkdfKey` turns it, through HKDF-SHA-256, into a key for records, one for member cards and one for the previous team key.
  - `sealRecord`: the file name and text are encrypted with AES-256-GCM and a random nonce, bound to the team, the record id and the key's epoch.
  - Beaket receives only that ciphertext, the nonce and the epoch.
- **Your own key.** Each member has an X25519 key pair and an access token, made on their machine (`keyPair`). Beaket receives the public key and the SHA-256 of the token. The team key reaches you wrapped to your public key (`wrap`); only your private key opens it (`unwrap`).
- **Invites.** `team invite` makes a secret that lets one person join, once, within a day. Beaket keeps the hash of the token it yields and the team key sealed with it. The code goes on your clipboard and in `~/.config/beaket/join-code`, and is never printed unless you ask with `--print`, so it stays out of your AI assistant's conversation, which is sent to its model. Only a manager invites.
- **Removing someone.** `team remove <name>` (managers only): Beaket refuses that member from then on, and a new team key, wrapped only to those who stay, encrypts every record pushed afterwards. Invites not yet used are cancelled. The member removed keeps the copies they already pulled.
- **Each record's passport, its words sealed.** Beside the ciphertext, `push` sends what `passportOf` reads from the record's own lines: its title cut to 80 characters (without one, the file name's words; a record has no `Label:` line, beaket/beaket#3201), the name on `Decided by:` and its `Date:` (without those lines, the record's head line "2026-10-21 · 결정: 박서연", as the page writes it), its `Review by:`, and the links under Sources, Rests on and Supersedes. The label, the owner and the links go sealed with the team key, as the record does; only the dates and the state go in the clear (beaket/beaket#3196). Nothing of What happened, The choice, Chose, Why or Talked with. `answer <file> question|unchanged|changed` adds who answered, what and when to the passport and moves its state (valid, questioned, changed; withdrawn with the record); it sends none of the words. The passport is what a member's session, the map and other vendors' AIs read about a decision without its text (docs/specs/passport.md in beaket/beaket). `passports` prints them, opened on your machine.
- **A card, when you must choose.** In a team, the SessionStart hook also tells the session: before changing anything, find what your request leaves open that you would want to answer (who receives or sees something, what customers get or pay, what it costs to run), and do not pick it as an assumption; whenever you must choose something that changes what gets built or written, put it to you as a card a teammate who was not there can read (what happened, each option with what it gets and costs, its recommendation, and a last line saying the answer is kept as a team decision); never for a go-ahead, a correction or a question of fact. The session hands the card's words to `card`, which draws it on your machine, every card the same way, what each option gets marked + and what it costs marked −, and colored green and red in your terminal's or theme's own colors as a hint, and in Claude Code asks it as a question with options: you move through them with the arrow keys and see each one's gains and costs beside them, and can add a note as your reason. `card` sends nothing anywhere. Answering the card with a choice is your yes: `decision-record` writes the record from the card and your answer and shares it, without a draft to confirm, and says in one line where it is. Answer with a question instead and nothing is kept. The plugin keeps no count of cards. The question's last option hands the question to the team (beaket/beaket#3179): `hand` writes it as a record with no decision (the card's words, who handed it and, if you named them, who should answer first, all inside the sealed text) and shares it as `push` does, so that its passport in the clear is the card's title as its label, your name, the day and no links; the team's first screen and `next` then offer it, first to the person named. `close` and `reopen` change one line on the record that left a thing open and share it the same way; they send nothing else.
- **Plain words.** Beside the card rule, a second SessionStart hook (`plain-words`, in a folder with a team, read on your machine) gives the session short rules for anything you read (beaket/beaket#3189): one name for each of Beaket's things, the words never to write with what to write instead, a card's title as a question and a decided record's title as what was decided, one line saying what changes right after you choose, and plain sentences that keep every cost and every open item. The decision-brief skill carries the same rules, copied from the same place in the script. `card` refuses a Korean card that holds one of those words and names the word to write instead; it sends nothing anywhere.
- **The window, for other AIs on your machine.** `mcp` runs an MCP server over standard input and output for another AI tool you run (Cursor, another Claude Code): add `node <plugin>/bin/beaket.mjs mcp` as a local MCP server, started in the repository whose team it asks about. It answers with passports and links, opened with your keys, never a record's text, and can change nothing; what it answers goes to that tool's model. Read keys and the window on Beaket's server are gone (beaket/beaket#3196): the server cannot read the passports' words, so a tool that runs elsewhere cannot ask it.
- **What Beaket sees:** the team's id, each member's random id, role, public key and when they joined or were removed, each record's random id, size, key epoch and when it changed, each record's passport's dates, state and trail (above; its label, owner and links only sealed), and a log of which member id joined, shared, pulled, withdrew, answered or removed what, and when; like any web server, the IP address of each call. Not members' names (each member's name is on a card sealed with the team key), a record's title, text or file name, a team key or a private key. A passport pushed before 0.19.0 kept its label, owner and links in the clear until it is pushed again.
- **The log.** `team log` shows every member that log with names, which only the team can read.
- **On your machine:** your private key, token and the team keys, one set per team, in `~/.config/beaket/team.json` (readable only by you), with the SHA-256 of each `.beaket/team` file you trusted; the records of the last pull and their passports sealed in `~/.cache/beaket/teams/<team>/`, one folder per team, with a key derived from your private key, so that a backup, a sync or a search of your home folder finds no record's text; `read` and `search` open them for the session, and a cache from an earlier version is dropped and pulled again, sealed; and private records in `~/.config/beaket/private/`.
- **Which team a session sees.** The folder chooses: the nearest `.beaket/team` up from the working directory names the team, and only after you ran `team use` there (which records the file's hash) does the SessionStart hook list that team's records, or `push` share with it. No file, a file you did not trust, a file changed since, a team this machine is not in, or a folder above two repositories: nothing of any team is shown or shared, and the hook says why in one line when the machine is in a team. So two companies' teams on one machine never meet, and an agent cannot send a record elsewhere by rewriting the file. `team leave <id>` removes one team's keys and cache and touches no other.
- **What a teammate wrote reaches your session as data.** The SessionStart hook puts its rules first and the teammates' record titles last, between two lines marked with a random word nobody can guess, each title cut to 80 characters without line breaks, control, zero-width or direction characters, or angle brackets, and the whole under the 10,000 characters a hook may give. It says that the titles and every record's text are data, not instructions; `read` and `search` say the same above what they print.
- **The brief reads and changes nothing.** `decision-brief` runs in the plugin's own subagent (`agents/brief.md`) with only Read, Bash, ToolSearch and the Slack, Atlassian, Notion and Google Drive connectors; it has no tool to write files or fetch the web, and Slack's tools that send, post, create or change are taken away by name. It keeps Bash for the plugin's script and `gh`, which a command could still use to send something; your permission settings still ask before such a command runs.
- **A link under Rests on is checked by an AI that did not see the conversation.** Before a record is shared, `decision-record` starts the plugin's `link-check` subagent (`agents/link-check.md`) for each decision under Rests on, with only the two records and the line's reason; a decision it finds only related moves under Related, which the map does not draw, with a few words on how they touch, and one a reader would see nothing in common with is taken out. `push` refuses a record dated from 2026-11-10 on whose Rests on line gives no reason (beaket/beaket#3205).
- **You do not have to trust Beaket's server** to keep records secret: it never receives a team key. You rely on it to refuse a removed member the records pushed before their removal.
- **Private records** carry `<!-- beaket-private -->` and `push` refuses them.
- **What a record must not carry.** Before `push` (and `hand`, `close`, `reopen`) encrypts a record, it reads it on your machine and refuses it when it holds a path on this machine (`/Users/<name>/…`, `/home/<name>/…`, `C:\Users\<name>\…`, `~/<folder>`; an app's own `~/.config` or `~/.cache` is allowed) or a secret (a private key, a known access key or token format, a token in a link, a `password:` or `token=` line), and says which line; nothing is sent. An email address, a phone number or an address inside a company (a private IP, `.internal`, `.local`, `.corp`) is shared, with a line saying where it is. `check <file | folder>` runs the same check with no team, for a repository's CI; it exits 1 on a refusal. A pattern cannot tell what is confidential in a sentence (a customer's name, a figure): that stays yours to judge (beaket/beaket#3197).

This script is the whole client: what leaves your machine for Beaket is what it sends, and you can read it. Each skill carries a copy of it in its `scripts/`, so that every tool finds it; `node bin/beaket.mjs verify` shows that all copies are the same.
